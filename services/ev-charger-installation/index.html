EV charger installation in Kanata.

Choosing where to charge at a Kanata property starts with where the vehicle parks and how exposed that spot is to rain and spray. Outdoor-rated equipment is a different purchase from a unit meant for a sheltered wall.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Kanata property

Photograph the parking position from the street side and from the garage door, and note where a post or wall would stand. Record which spots stay uncovered during rain, drifting leaves or splashing from the driveway.

Ask whether the proposed equipment is rated for the exposure at that exact spot, and how the cable would be protected along its route. Ask also how the route would change if the unit went on an attached garage wall instead.

What should the assessment establish?

A vehicle parks in the open at a Kanata property, so a charger there would face the weather. The exact mounting position is noted, with the exposure it meets and the reason the chosen equipment suits it. Protection for the cable and any enclosure is described beside that. The contractor weighs the charging load against the existing supply and says whether a dedicated circuit is proposed. The capacity reasoning appears in plain terms, not as a bare conclusion. Equipment, mounting position and cable route are named in the scope, together with the party responsible for the ESA notification.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a charger in an open parking spot need different equipment?

It can. Where the unit sits outside, ESA advises outdoor-rated equipment, and the contractor should say how the unit, the mounting and the cable are protected. Compare that with a sheltered garage wall before choosing.

Can I plan the charger location before I take possession?

Yes. Sketch the parking position and the likely route in words, then ask the seller for any drawings or earlier ESA paperwork. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can confirm the route and the supply once access is available.
