A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Kanata property

Write down which rooms are affected, whether the garage lights join in and which large appliances were running when it happened. Stop using any device that gives off heat or smells unusual, and never open a cover to look.

Ask how the contractor will define the diagnostic scope, which circuits and connections it covers and what the written findings will say. Ask also whether the seller disclosure or inspection report should be shared before the visit.

What should the assessment establish?

Rooms, devices and timing, as you recorded them when lights dimmed in one area of a Kanata house, are the evidence the report builds on. Its account of the installation names the parts that were examined and those that sit outside the agreed scope. The cause comes next, identified and explained rather than assumed from the age of the equipment, ahead of any repair approval. A short section on what could not be reached tells you how far the findings go. Observations and recommendations stand apart in the report, which lets you compare repair proposals fairly.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does flickering in one room mean the whole house has a problem?

Not necessarily, and it should not be guessed at. The pattern of rooms, devices and timing is the useful evidence, and the contractor decides how far the diagnostic scope needs to reach.

What should I do if a flickering light also feels warm?

Stop using that fixture or device, leave it alone and do not open anything. Note what you noticed and when, then describe it to a Licensed Electrical Contractor so the assessment can start from your observations.
